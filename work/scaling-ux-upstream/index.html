Leadership case

Scaling UX upstream

Moving experience design from late-stage review into the product decisions that shape complex enterprise software.

EvidenceBriefPrototypeQuality gate
ScopeCross-domain portfolio
RoleUX practice builder
ProofCapability scaled from 2 to 28

Context

A global industrial technology portfolio was growing its software capability across multiple product teams. The products were complex, the domain knowledge was specialized, and decisions made early in the lifecycle had a lasting effect on delivery quality.

Challenge

UX often entered after requirements were locked and implementation decisions had already narrowed the solution. That reduced the room to test assumptions, understand user needs, or correct workflow problems before development effort accumulated.

Decision

Shift UX involvement upstream. Bring discovery, stakeholder alignment, persona and job-to-be-done thinking, journey evidence, early prototypes, and validation into product definition before development commitment.

System

The approach connected discovery workshops, design briefs, prototypes, validation, user stories, acceptance criteria, design QA, and feedback loops. The aim was not a one-off intervention. It was a repeatable model that product teams could use while making decisions.

Leadership value

This changed the role of UX from a late-stage interface reviewer into an earlier product partner. It also created a clearer foundation for coaching, shared practices, and quality conversations across teams.

Outcomes and value

What this work changed

Earlier influence

UX moved into inception and pre-development decisions, where teams still had room to shape the product direction.

Scaled capability

The cross-domain UX capability grew from 2 to 28 people.

Reusable quality model

Shared discovery and review practices made the approach useful beyond a single product team.

Value: The operating model reduces late rework risk, improves alignment between product and engineering, and keeps customer evidence close to the decisions that create delivery cost.
