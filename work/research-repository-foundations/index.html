Enablement case

Research repository foundations

Organizing research so personas, journeys, tasks, and field evidence remain useful after the workshop ends.

ObserveStructureConnectReuse
ScopePortfolio research
FormatRepository model
FocusEvidence reuse

Context

Enterprise-product research accumulates across interviews, usability tests, customer visits, stakeholder conversations, journey work, and market learning. Without structure, useful evidence becomes difficult to retrieve.

Challenge

A folder of reports does not create a decision system. Teams need to connect raw observations with personas, job-to-be-done evidence, journey stages, product contexts, and the operational flows where friction appears.

Decision

Organize research around reusable experience context: journeys, personas, products, operational phases, tasks, and ecosystem relationships. Keep new evidence connected to those anchors as the portfolio evolves.

System

The model links qualitative research storage, journey mapping, and quantitative validation. It supports tagged study outputs, journey-based context, recurring research inputs, and retrieval for design and planning decisions.

Leadership value

The repository approach helps research survive the project that created it. It also gives product teams a stronger base for requirement discussions, concept work, and continuous learning.

Outcomes and value

What this work changed

Reusable learning

Past interviews, tests, and field observations remain easier to retrieve for later decisions.

Broader decision support

The model connects qualitative insight, journey framing, and quantitative validation instead of treating them as separate activities.

Value: A research repository becomes valuable when it reduces repeated discovery and helps teams make better decisions with evidence that already exists.
