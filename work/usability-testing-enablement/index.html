Enablement case

Usability-testing enablement

Making usability testing easier to prepare, run, and reuse through practical guidance for designers and researchers.

PurposeParticipantsSessionsSynthesis
ScopeProduct teams
FormatPlaybooks and guide
FocusEvidence quality

Context

Product teams need usability testing at different moments: early concept feedback, workflow evaluation, pre-release review, and focused validation of risky assumptions.

Challenge

Without clear preparation, teams can begin with an incomplete prototype, unclear tasks, the wrong participant profile, weak stakeholder alignment, or inconsistent documentation. Those gaps reduce the value of the evidence.

Decision

Create complementary guidance for designers and researchers, supported by a preparation checklist. Clarify when to test, how to frame objectives, how to recruit participants, and how to keep moderation neutral.

System

The guide covers request setup, purpose and tasks, stakeholder alignment, participant roles and experience, prototype readiness, sensitive-data masking, technical setup, observation, synthesis, and research-repository capture.

Leadership value

The playbooks reduce method ambiguity. They make it easier for product teams to ask for the right support and easier for researchers to preserve evidence quality.

Outcomes and value

What this work changed

Repeatable preparation

Teams have a practical checklist for preparing a usable prototype, tasks, participants, and session setup.

Clearer roles

Designers, researchers, and product stakeholders have a more explicit shared process.

Reusable evidence

The guidance connects session outputs to structured synthesis and repository capture.

Value: Better preparation produces more credible evidence and helps teams use testing as a decision tool rather than a late-stage approval ritual.

Role-based playbooks

Public summary. Internal process links, templates, and product-specific studies remain private.
