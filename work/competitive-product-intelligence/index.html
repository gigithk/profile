Leadership case

Competitive product intelligence

Building a repeatable evaluation method that turns competitor workflows into product-strategy input and prioritized opportunities.

ScopeEnterprise software

Context

Competitive evaluation had traditionally focused on hardware. As software became more important to product strategy, teams needed a clearer way to understand competing digital experiences and decide where future UX effort should concentrate.

Challenge

An earlier software-review activity had struggled to produce insights that stakeholders could use. A collection of screens or feature comparisons was not enough. The work needed to connect core workflows, user journeys, experience gaps, service-model observations, and product priorities.

Reframed goal

Create a reusable evaluation method that turns competitor analysis into decision-ready product insight. The method should help leaders see the big picture while still giving product teams concrete areas for further evaluation.

My role

I led the framework work, starting with a cross-functional workshop involving product, domain, and architecture perspectives. I shaped the evaluation process, connected the UX analysis with stakeholder questions, and designed the approach so it could be handed over to additional designers.

Leadership value

The framework made competitive intelligence useful as a product-strategy input rather than a one-time report. It created a repeatable learning method that could move between products, support prioritization, and broaden the contribution of UX in system-level decisions.

Reusable framework

From competitor review to decision-ready insight.

The evaluation method keeps the analysis focused on comparable workflows and the decisions stakeholders need to make. It moves from market context to experience evidence, then translates findings into priority areas.

  1. 01

    Select the focus

    Start with the product context and the strategic questions that need a clearer answer.

  2. 02

    Choose comparators

    Identify relevant competitors using market presence, sales context, and local or global relevance.

  3. 03

    Map core workflows

    Isolate comparable workflows so the review follows meaningful user journeys rather than disconnected screens.

  4. 04

    Evaluate the experience

    Combine journey mapping and expert review to identify experience patterns, pitfalls, and service-model observations.

  5. 05

    Frame priorities

    Translate the evidence into findings and priority areas that can inform product-strategy conversations.

Decision outputs

Journey-level map

A big-picture view of comparable workflows and user-experience gaps.

Benchmark findings

Structured observations that make differences easier to discuss.

Service-model observations

Evidence beyond interface comparison, including how the experience is shaped.

Priority areas

A clearer starting point for future product and implementation decisions.

Outcomes and value

What this work changed

Continued investment

Broader UX contribution

Value: Structured competitive intelligence helps product teams make clearer priority decisions and keeps market learning connected to implementation conversations.
