The first design challenge was not a screen. It was creating the shared process that would make every later screen more grounded and buildable.

SENTRON powermanager helps teams monitor energy consumption in low-voltage power distribution. The product was being redeveloped on Siemens' in-house Desigo CC platform, bringing existing functionality into a new technical environment while improving usability within platform constraints.

I joined the Electrical Products team in September 2018 and led the UX work through March 2022. The immediate challenge was broader than interface design: the product had no established design process, its user segments needed definition, and the team needed a consistent way to document requirements for development.

How I contributed

I led persona discovery, design-process definition, requirement definition and the design activities for the product from September 2018 to March 2022.

My role connected facilitation and hands-on product design: bring the right disciplines into the room, turn the discussion into documented requirements and wireframes, then review the developed result before it moved forward.

  • Establish a design process compatible with the team's Lean-Agile delivery model
  • Define user segments and create reusable personas with stakeholders
  • Facilitate cross-functional design workshops for functional requirements
  • Document user stories, task cases, acceptance criteria and wireframes
  • Create a UX review checkpoint before developed work entered a code branch

Frame the challenge

A mature product was moving onto a new platform.

SENTRON powermanager supports energy monitoring in low-voltage power distribution, including reporting and role-specific views. The new product was planned on the Desigo CC platform.

The programme needed to migrate existing powermanager functionality, improve usability within platform constraints, and establish a design process the team could repeat across releases.

Before designing features, the team needed a way to design together.

There was no established design process for powermanager. The user segments and their use of the software were not yet clearly defined, and requirement documentation needed a consistent structure covering user stories, acceptance criteria and wireframes.

I had three months to put those foundations in place so the next release cycle could begin with clearer inputs.

Platform migration could not become a feature-copying exercise.

Moving existing functionality to a new platform created technical constraints, but it also created a moment to clarify who the product served and how requirements would be translated into usable behaviour.

Without a shared process, product, domain, architecture, UX and engineering could each carry a different interpretation of the same feature into delivery.

Three months, a new domain and a fixed platform context.

I was new to energy management and the electrical domain. At the same time, persona discovery, process definition and documentation standards all had to progress in parallel within a three-month setup window.

The process also had to fit the Lean-Agile model already used by the delivery team and respect the constraints of Desigo CC.

Build understanding

Treat process, documentation and personas as one system.

I planned the work across three connected tracks: define the user segments, establish a design process that could support future releases, and define how requirements would be documented.

Running the tracks in parallel meant that the personas could inform feature discussions, while the new workshop and documentation model gave that evidence a path into delivery.

Build the user picture with the people closest to the product.

I facilitated a two-hour virtual workshop in Conceptboard with five to six participants drawn from sales and business development, product management and customer support.

The group identified user segments, created persona perspectives in smaller teams, compared them and agreed a final direction. The resulting segmentation and persona became reusable product context.

A persona becomes useful when it changes the requirement conversation.

The persona work was not separated from delivery. It gave the cross-functional team a shared picture of who a feature was for while requirements were still being discussed.

  • Domain stakeholders held different pieces of the user context.
  • Facilitation made those perspectives comparable and decidable.
  • Documentation carried the agreed context into development.

Make the key decisions

Create a continuous loop inside every release cycle.

Functional requirements entered from the product backlog and moved into a design workshop with the architect, product owner, UX designer and domain expert. Together we discussed the requirement and produced the design document.

That document connected the user story, acceptance criteria and wireframes. Developed work then passed through a UX design review before being approved for check-in to a branch.

Give every discipline a clear moment to contribute.

The operating model made three decisions explicit: who needed to shape a requirement, what evidence and definition had to be captured, and when UX approval was required before code progressed.

Shape the solution

Make the design decision buildable and reviewable.

The design document became the bridge between discussion and implementation. It recorded the user story, task case, acceptance criteria and relevant wireframes in one place.

This gave developers clearer behaviour to build and gave UX a concrete reference for the later review checkpoint.

Outcome and reflection

The product gained a process it could repeat.

The team established a repeatable design process for the product, a clearer shared understanding of functional requirements and a reusable user segmentation and persona.

The supplied case-study source records releases from V4.0 through V5.1 during my tenure. It does not provide quantitative usability or business metrics, so none are claimed here.

The value extended beyond one feature or one release.

Workshops were planned for functional features so the team could reach shared clarity before development. The persona and segmentation could be reused, while the process provided a consistent structure for later release work.

The source also states that the process was later adopted across projects in the portfolio. The extent of that adoption should be confirmed before making a broader public claim.

In complex products, designing the collaboration is part of designing the product.

A good enterprise experience depends on more than an interface. The persona, requirement, workshop, documentation and review model determine whether user context survives the journey from an idea to working software.
