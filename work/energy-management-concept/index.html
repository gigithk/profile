Product case

Energy-management concept exploration

Translating specialized domain knowledge into a clearer product concept for managing energy demand with less manual effort.

Domain insightRulesPrioritiesConcept
ScopeEnergy management
FormatConcept exploration
FocusLow-touch operations

Context

Energy-demand decisions involve operational constraints, varying load priorities, time-based conditions, and cost considerations. The product concept needed to make that complexity more understandable for the people responsible for day-to-day decisions.

Challenge

The domain logic is specialized. Users need to distinguish critical, important, and non-essential loads while responding to schedules, contract limits, peak conditions, and tariff changes without constant manual intervention.

Decision

Use domain workshops, persona thinking, and job-to-be-done evidence to frame a concept around rule-based decisions, prioritized loads, notifications, and clearer information architecture.

System

The concept organizes load categories, decision rules, schedule conditions, limits, alerts, and mobile access into a more coherent operating model. The goal is a proactive experience that supports expert decisions without demanding continuous monitoring.

Leadership value

The work demonstrates how UX can translate technical domain complexity into a product concept that is easier to discuss, validate, and evolve.

Outcomes and value

What this work changed

Clearer concept architecture

Domain inputs were translated into an understandable model of priorities, rules, and notifications.

Decision-focused interaction model

The concept keeps operational choices visible while reducing unnecessary manual effort.

Progressed exploration

The work advanced beyond the initial idea stage and created a stronger basis for further evaluation.

Value: The concept aims to make complex energy decisions more accessible, proactive, and cost-aware without flattening the underlying domain logic.
