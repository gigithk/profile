What fourteen years taught me

These are not principles I started with. They are lessons I have collected while navigating complex products, difficult trade-offs, research, leadership and now AI.

  1. 01
    I start by listening.

    Before opening Figma, I want to understand the people doing the work, the decisions they face and the constraints the product cannot ignore.

  2. 02
    I want UX in the room early.

    The best time to influence an experience is while the problem, priorities and requirements can still change—not after they have hardened.

  3. 03
    I draw the mess.

    When a system feels complicated, I map it. Making workflows, dependencies and competing needs visible gives teams something real to decide around.

  4. 04
    I prototype what makes me nervous.

    The uncertain interaction or uncomfortable assumption is usually more valuable to test than the screen everyone already agrees on.

  5. 05
    Research has to move something.

    A polished report is not the outcome. Research earns its place when it changes a direction, a priority or the confidence behind a decision.

  6. 06
    I design beyond the screen.

    A good interface can still fail inside a broken journey or operating model. I pay as much attention to relationships as I do to the surface.

  7. 07
    I use AI to think wider, not to decide for me.

    AI helps me explore faster, challenge familiar patterns and build ambitious experiments. Judgment, context and accountability remain human work.

  8. 08
    I design for the day after launch.

    The work must hold up through technical constraints, imperfect content, organizational trade-offs, accessibility needs and actual everyday use.
